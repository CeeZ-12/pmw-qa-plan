The idea

When a new site is ready or a live site is edited, a bot checks every page for common problems and posts a short summary in Slack. The QA team reviews it and decides what is a real issue.

Why:

  • Less time on boring, repetitive checks (broken links, missing page titles, image sizes).
  • Problems are caught before the client sees them.
  • Results are in one place, so everyone sees the same thing.

We are not starting from zero — we already have most of the pieces. The plan connects them and makes them reliable.

The tools we already have

Four tools, already built by the team.

Speed Checker

Checks how fast sites load every Monday and posts results to Slack. Ask the PMW Speed Bot to check any site.

Analytics Health Monitor

Checks every day that Google Analytics and Tag Manager are installed and working on each site.

Site Audit tool

Visits every page of a site and finds broken links, missing titles and headings, accessibility problems and oversized images. Run by hand today.

NestHub Emulator

Lets a developer preview a site on their own computer before uploading changes. Stays a developer tool.

How it will work

The goal at the end of the steps:

  1. Someone asks for a check in Slack — when a new site is ready on its nesthub.com address, or after a live site is edited.
  2. The bot visits the site like a normal visitor and checks every main page. It needs no logins.
  3. A short summary appears in Slack with a link to the full report. Only new problems are listed.
  4. The QA team reviews it and decides what needs fixing. The report link can be added to the site's Monday item.

Ground rules

  • The bot only looks — it never changes a website.
  • People decide. The bot flags possible problems; the QA team confirms them.
  • No fake leads. The bot never submits forms. A person tests forms once, on the live site after launch, clearly marked as a test.
  • No spam. One Slack message per check, listing only what's new.
  • One step at a time. Each step is finished and working before the next starts.

Next

Checklist

  • Turn the answers into a list of checks, sorted by importance

Everyone on the QA team

3

Make the Site Audit tool faster and more accurate

Not started

A test on a small site took about 2 hours, and most "broken links" it found were false alarms (social media sites blocking bots). We fix that first.

Checklist

  • Check each link once per site, not once per page
  • Stop false alarms from social media and government sites
  • Group repeated problems ("same issue on 20 pages" = one item)
  • Mark each problem as Critical, High, Medium or Low
  • Report security certificate errors and missing pages

Who

Developers

Done when: a typical site is checked in under 20 minutes with very few false alarms.
4

Ask for a check from Slack

Not started

Like the PMW Speed Bot today: mention the bot with a site address, and the summary comes back in the same thread.

Checklist

  • Add a "QA this site" command to the Slack bot
  • Run the check automatically in GitHub
  • Post one short summary with a link to the full report

Who

Developers and automation analysts

Done when: it works on 5 sites without anyone opening a terminal.
5

Try it on real sites

Not started

For 2–4 weeks, the QA team uses the bot on real launches and edits and tells us when it's right or wrong.

Checklist

  • Use it on new launches and live-site edits
  • Mark each flagged problem "real" or "false alarm"
  • Remove or fix checks that cause too many false alarms

Who

QA team

Done when: the team agrees with most of what the bot flags.
6

Add checks for PMW-specific problems

Not started

Problems we have seen before on our own sites, which general tools miss.

Checklist

  • Leftover placeholder text like [Market] or [Company Name]
  • Pages still linking to the nesthub.com address after launch
  • Contact details missing or broken
  • Scripts or fonts loaded twice
  • Missing or repeated page titles and descriptions
  • Ideas from the QA team's answers (step 2)

Who

Developers, with QA team input

Done when: the bot catches these problems on a test site.
7

Compare before and after

Not started

After an edit, the report shows what got worse and what got fixed compared with the last check.

Checklist

  • Keep a history of checks for each site
  • Show "new", "still there" and "fixed"
  • Add the report link to the site's Monday item

Who

Developers and automation analysts

Done when: an edit's report clearly shows what changed.

Later ideas

Only after the 7 steps work well: comparing screenshots before and after a change, pulling in client feedback from Pastel, comparing pages with Figma designs, and AI-written summaries.

Questions for the QA team

Short answers are fine. Reply in the Slack thread with the question number.

How you check sites today

  1. What do you check before a new site launches? (Share your checklist, even a rough one.)
  2. What do you check after a live site is edited?
  3. About how long does QA take for a new site? And after an edit?
  4. What tools do you use now? (Browser extensions, scripts, spreadsheets, anything.)

What goes wrong

  1. What problems slip through most often, or get noticed by the client first?
  2. Which checks are the most repetitive for you?
  3. Are there NestHub or template problems that keep coming back?

Priorities

  1. What must be fixed before a site can go live?
  2. What can wait until after launch?

Reports

  1. If a bot posted QA results in Slack, what would you want to see? What would make you ignore it?
  2. Where should results go: Slack, Monday, a dashboard, or a mix?

Forms and launch day

  1. How do you test contact and lead forms today, and who lets the client know?
  2. What do you check on launch day, after the site moves off its nesthub.com address?

Trying it out

  1. Would you try the bot on a few real sites and tell us when it's wrong?
  2. Anything else we should know, or a tool we've missed?

Words we use

Bot
The automatic checker. It opens web pages like a visitor would, in a browser no one sees.
GitHub
Where the tools are stored. It keeps every version, and can run the checks on its own computers.
n8n
The automation tool that connects Slack, GitHub and Monday — it already runs the PMW Speed Bot.
False alarm
Something the bot flags that isn't actually a problem.
Build address
The something.nesthub.com address where a site is built before it moves to the client's own domain.
Accessibility
Whether people using screen readers, keyboards or low vision can use the site.
